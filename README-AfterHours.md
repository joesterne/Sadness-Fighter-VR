# After Hours

A Meta Quest game about finding room for yourself after losing a job. One build runs on Quest 2, Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S, with tracked hands first and controllers as soon as you pick them up.

Open `Assets/AfterHours/Scenes/AfterHours.unity` in Unity 6000.6.3f1 and press Play. The `After Hours` menu can rebuild the scene or build a Quest APK. The original sample scene is retained.

## What is playable

- **The lobby**: choose a chapter in any order. Detailed office memories sit behind glass. The surrounding spaces use a midnight/teal palette with restrained coral and brass accents.
- **Ocean of shame**: fourteen strokes bring a lighthouse and welcoming shore closer. Two oars rest in oarlocks on the sides of the boat. Grab one by the handle, push it forward and draw it back: the oar turns about its lock, so the blade sweeps the other way through the water, as a real oar does. Or select the accessible ROW button, which sweeps both oars by itself. The lighthouse light turns clockwise, long swells roll across the water toward the shore, a wave lifts, crests and slides away in the sound each time one passes, the surf on the island grows louder as you row nearer, gulls cry and the sea laps at the hull. Short fades move the scenery while keeping the boat and player stable. **OCEAN AT NIGHT** in the menu turns the sea to a starlit night with a moon, a stronger beam and a bell buoy.
- **Heavy things**: five named feelings—anger, fear, relief, grief, uncertainty—wait in front of you. Pick a box up (pinch with hands; squeeze the trigger or grip with controllers). Then either point at the truck and let go, and the box glides into the processing bay, or **throw it**: let go while your hand is moving and the box leaves with your hand's speed and direction. A throw aimed roughly at the truck is bent in toward it, so even a short seated flick lands in the bay, where the box is caught and drawn into its place. A throw the other way flies where you sent it, and a box that stops short of the truck goes back on its stand a moment later, with a gentle message and no penalty. Point-and-let-go works as before, so the chapter can still be finished from a seat with a still hand. Every feeling is accepted; processing is not disposal. There is a small secret in this room: hold a box in each hand and slam them together, and both burst in a flash, a spray of sparks, a puff of smoke and a shower of chips in the boxes' own colours, with a boom, and are back on their stands about two seconds later. Nothing is counted, so the chapter is untouched.
- **A small spill**: the coffee machine leaks and additional people watch with each mistake, up to eight. A low murmur grows with the crowd. ASK FOR HELP is available immediately, releases the pressure fault, dismisses the audience and quiets the room. Pick up your cup, point under the spout and let go, then brew three small pours, with BREW or by pulling the lever on the side of the machine. **KITCHEN CROWD** in the menu hides the crowd without changing the task. The machine has a lever, a gauge, a portafilter, a steam valve and a steam wand: point at the wand or the valve for a hiss and a plume of steam (it works even while the pressure is faulty). Three more mugs stand beside your cup on the counter, a teal, a coral and a navy one, and any of them can be carried, sent under the spout or set down. They can also be thrown: a mug thrown or dropped from carrying height shatters into shards and dust (and a splash of coffee if it was full) with a crash, and is back on its shelf whole and empty about two and a half seconds later, so nothing is lost and nothing is counted. Setting one down gently never breaks it. The six cupboards under the counter open when you point at a door and press, and one of them, the fifth from the left, hides a small secret: an orange mug. Take it, put it under the spout and brew, and the Orange mug pin is yours.
- **The infinite archive**: sort six notes into FACT, FEAR, and STILL TRUE: pick one up, point at its tray and let go. The sorting desk stands on a railed reading floor with no walls: past a colonnade, stacks of shelving rise out of sight and run on in every direction, with catwalks, bridges and small warm lamps, until a deep blue haze takes them. Loose pages drift down through the aisles. A note sent to the wrong tray drifts back to the desk for another try, without penalties.
- **The old résumé**: five old résumé pages stand on a desk: a job title, "always available", "exceeded every target", ten years at one company, and a reason for leaving left blank. The shredder stands on your side of the desk, a step or two to the right of where you arrive and turned to face you. Pick a page up, point at the shredder and let it go, or throw it in: a throw aimed roughly at the shredder is helped the rest of the way, like the boxes into the truck. Each page is drawn into the slot, a flurry of loose bits of paper bursts up out of the head and tumbles down across the whole room, the paper in the bin grows, and a line answers it ("Your worth was never a quarterly number."). Every page leaves its scraps where they landed: over the floor (thickest around the shredder and the desk), on the desk, on the shredder's top and on the window sill, so the room fills with paper as the pages go. When the last page is gone, a blank page asks you to keep one line that is still true about you: *I learn fast*, *I care about people*, *I keep going*, or *I make things better*. The line you choose goes on the mirror in the lobby. There is a small secret in this room: throw a page onto the floor instead and it bursts into a shower of multi-coloured confetti with a pop and a flutter of paper, and is back on its stand a moment later. Nothing is counted, so the chapter is untouched.
- **The rage room**: the old work computer sits on a desk in a bare room with plywood on the wall: monitor, keyboard, tower and mouse, the inbox still open ("Your access has been revoked"). Point at the bat beside the desk and pinch: it comes to your hand and stays there until you put it back. Swing at the computer. A short flick of the forearm counts as a full hit, so the whole room plays from a seat, and a slow push through a part does nothing. Each part breaks in stages, with its own sound, a jolt and flying pieces: the screen cracks and argues back ("ARE YOU SURE? Unsaved feelings will be lost."), then goes dark and falls; keys fly and the keyboard snaps in two; the tower dents, loses its side panel and fan in sparks, and topples; the mouse is flattened, then split. A line answers each part as it goes. Every hit sends pieces flying, thrown away from you: the computer sheds 152 loose pieces in all (each key and the space bar, screws, glass, circuit boards, a graphics card, a hard drive, memory sticks, the fan and heat sink, cables), and the floor ends up covered. Five desk things break too, a mug, a desk phone, a stapler, a pen cup and a name plate (58 more pieces, 210 in all), but they are extras and do not count toward the chapter. A piece that has landed can be batted again: swing at one on the desk or the floor and it pops up and skids the way the bat was going, with a small clatter. When everything is broken, the room's hum fades to silence, a panel says it is allowed to be angry about losing something that mattered, and the chapter completes. **WHEEL IN A NEW ONE** brings back a whole computer to go again, and **PUT THE BAT BACK** returns the bat to its rack.
- **The ambush**: the seventh chapter, about a sudden meeting where you are let go, and the confusion and anger after it. You arrive in complete darkness. A few seconds later the lights stutter on, cold and harsh, and you are sitting at one end of a very long boardroom table. Across it two clowns, one coral and one teal, stand nearly three times life size, leaning in over you, smiling a smile that never changes and saying hollow things on a screen between them (“Thank you for coming in.” “This isn't personal.”). Everything is done from the seat, by pointing. **Name your feelings**: six cards lie on the table (CONFUSED, BLINDSIDED, HURT, ANGRY, I WANT A REASON, WORRIED ABOUT WHAT'S NEXT). Each one you name earns a kind line and makes both clowns smaller and sillier (a propeller beanie after two, a bowtie after four) until they are smaller than a person and have nothing left to say. **Talk back**: five replies climb a staircase on the left of the table, from WAIT. WHAT JUST HAPPENED? to I AM FURIOUS., and a clown answers each with the kind of thing a company says (“We have a form for that.”). They do not count toward the chapter. **Throw the paperwork**: five sheets (a termination notice, an exit interview, a confidentiality agreement, a restructuring plan and an end to benefits) and a thick severance packet lie on the right of the table. Carry one, point at a clown and let go, and it flies to the clown's chest and the clown flinches; or toss one; or shake one hard from side to side and it tears into scraps. A page is back on the table a moment later, so nothing is ever lost. Six balloons float above the clowns and can be popped, and each clown has a nose to honk. When all six feelings are named, **STAND UP** appears in front of you: the chair rolls back, the light turns warm, the clowns are small and quiet and a door to the lobby opens. That completes the chapter and earns the Stood up pin. **GENTLE AMBUSH** in the menu starts the clowns smaller (1.9 times life size, not 2.8) and upright, and **DARK OPENING** (on by default) can be switched off to arrive with the lights already on. Opening the menu, or doing anything in the room, while it is still dark brings the lights up at once.
- **Room for tomorrow**: a rooftop for rest, available from the beginning. It is the brightest, most colourful place in the game, built to feel like a toy city at sunrise: a sky that runs from gold at the horizon through peach and pink to morning blue, a big glowing sun, soft pink clouds that drift across and a striped hot-air balloon that bobs and turns, a skyline of chunky pastel buildings with lit windows, rooftop water towers and aerials all around, strings of coloured bunting over the deck, a striped rug, two benches, two deck chairs with a side table and a glass of water, potted plants, flower boxes, a little stairwell hut with a blue door and a wooden water tower, and four beanbags to wander between. The sign on its board and the layout are as they were. Completing all seven chapters changes the ending to “You are more than your job.”

Completed chapters and partial progress persist locally between launches. Every oar stroke, delivered box, sorted note, coffee step, shredded page, broken computer part and named feeling saves automatically. Loose objects return to their starting positions when the app reopens; accepted items stay processed. CONTINUE in the lobby returns to the last room. **TRY AGAIN** in the menu resets the current puzzle without deleting chapter completion. A crossing of the ocean that you finished starts again from open water the next time you go in (the chapter stays marked as done); one still under way carries on where you left it. Nothing is timed, and there is no minimum session length.

**Background saving.** Besides saving after each small step, the game now saves by itself in the background: a snapshot is taken as you leave a room, another as you arrive in the next, and a quiet one about every 45 seconds in between. Nothing is written unless something has changed since the last save, so saving costs no hitch at a door or in the middle of a room. After each room change a faint "Saved" shows in the upper right of your view for about a second and fades; the quiet timed saves show nothing, the cue steps aside while the menu is open, and CLEAN MODE never shows it. The game also keeps a second copy of your progress (the checkpoint, the chapters finished, the wardrobe and your comfort settings) as a small file, `AfterHours-save.json`, in the game's own folder on the headset. If a fresh install finds no progress of its own but finds that file, it puts the progress back before the first scene loads, and it never replaces progress it already has; a damaged file, or one with anything but the game's own saved values in it, is ignored. Android removes that folder when an app is uninstalled, so the file only survives a reinstall because the installer in the editor (After Hours > Install release APK on connected Quest) copies it off the headset before it uninstalls a differently signed copy and puts it back before the game starts. An ordinary update over the same signature keeps everything anyway. Uninstalling from the headset's own library still removes the game's folder and the progress with it.

## First visit

The first time the game opens, a small card in the lobby shows a content note, then teaches the two things you need by having you do them once:

1. **Before you begin**: After Hours is about losing a job: the shame, the worry, and what is still true. It is a gentle experience, not a substitute for professional support, and you can take a break whenever you like.
2. **Point and pinch**: select the card's button.
3. **Your menu**: open the menu (left palm pinch, or the controller's menu button). The card moves on by itself once the menu opens.
4. **Begin anywhere**: choose a door, or CONTINUE. Small steps also earn things to wear at the mirror.

SKIP closes the card at any step, and walking through a door counts as finishing it. It doesn't appear again.

## The mirror and your wardrobe

A full-length mirror is built into the lobby's left wall. **MIRROR** on the desk (under CONTINUE) brings you to the spot in front of it, facing the glass, so a seated player never has to walk there. The sign also counts any new pieces waiting for you.

Your reflection is a faceted figure in the game's style. It copies your head, and your hands or controllers, mirrored as a real mirror would: lean in and it leans in, raise your right hand and it raises the hand on your right. With no hands tracked, its arms rest at its sides. It is drawn without a second camera: the room behind the glass is a mirror-image copy of this end of the lobby, and the figure's parent is flipped across the glass. Only the lobby is ever active, so it costs nothing in the other rooms.

**A small secret.** Tap the glass three times, each tap within a couple of seconds of the last, and your reflection becomes an X-ray: a white, glowing skeleton on a dark backdrop that follows your head, hands and height exactly as the reflection does. Three more taps bring you back, and so does leaving the lobby. It is not saved and it does nothing to the game.

## Outfit add-ons (in-app purchases)

After Hours is meant to be free to play. Every chapter, every comfort option and every earned piece stays free. What is sold is extra outfit pieces, one by one, as add-ons on the Meta Horizon Store. Every outfit slot has a free starter piece, so nobody ever has to buy anything.

**The catalog.** 23 add-ons, each with its own look on the reflection. The SKU of each is `outfit_` and its id with underscores (the hoodie is `outfit_midnight_hoodie`). The suggested prices are only a starting point: the dashboard sets the real ones.

| Slot | Add-ons (id) | Suggested price |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Midnight hoodie (`midnight-hoodie`), Sea glass polo (`polo`), Harbour flannel (`flannel`), Lilac turtleneck (`turtleneck`), Sunshine stripe tee (`stripe-tee`) | $1.99 |
| Hat | Sky bucket hat (`bucket-hat`), Flat cap (`flat-cap`), Wide sun hat (`sun-hat`), Cosy headphones (`headphones`) | $0.99 |
| Legs: trousers | Blue jeans (`jeans`), Leaf cargo trousers (`cargo`), Teal joggers (`joggers`), Corduroys (`cords`) | $1.99 |
| Legs: skirts | Pleated skirt (`pleated-skirt`), Coral A-line skirt (`coral-skirt`), Sea glass midi skirt (`midi-skirt`) | $1.99 |
| Gloves | Knit gloves (`knit-gloves`), Work gloves (`work-gloves`), Garden gloves (`garden-gloves`) | $0.99 |
| Shoes | White trainers (`trainers`), Work boots (`boots`), Brass loafers (`loafers`), Sunshine wellies (`wellies`) | $1.49 |

The full list with SKUs, ready to type into the dashboard, is in `Documentation/AddOns-catalog.tsv`. Trousers and skirts share the LEGS row, so you wear one or the other.

**Trying on and buying.** At the mirror, the outfit rows step through what you own and then the add-ons on sale. Stepping onto an add-on you don't own only tries it on: the reflection wears it, the row shows its price and IN THE STORE, and a coral **BUY** button covers the status board under the right-hand wardrobe, naming the piece and its price. Several pieces can be tried on at once, and BUY is for the latest one. BUY opens Meta's own checkout. Once Meta confirms, the piece is yours and goes on at once. A closed checkout buys nothing, and you can keep trying it on. Nothing tried on is saved, and leaving the lobby takes off everything that was only being tried on. The add-ons appear only once the store has listed them, so a headset that can't reach the store shows just what you own.

**How it works.** At launch `AddOnShop` (in `Scripts/AddOns.cs`) starts Meta's Platform SDK (`com.meta.xr.sdk.platform`) with the game's app id. It runs the store's ownership (entitlement) check, puts back every add-on the player already owns, and fetches the price of each add-on. A bought piece is an ordinary wardrobe piece. It never counts toward the twelve pieces to earn and never shows on the STILL TO FIND board. The wardrobe keeps its own copy of what was bought (`bought` in `AfterHours.Wardrobe.v1`), so add-ons show at once and offline. When the store can be reached, it decides: a refunded piece is taken off, and the free piece for that slot goes back on. Offline, the headset's own cache of purchases is used, and it can only add pieces.

**Bundles.** `Outfits.Bundles` can sell several add-ons under one SKU (for example a whole look for less), and `AddOnShop.BuyBundle` buys one. There are none yet. A bundle needs its own durable add-on in the dashboard, and a button somewhere to buy it.

The store sits behind an interface, `IAddOnStore`. On a Quest it is `MetaAddOnStore`. In the editor it is `TestAddOnStore`, which sells every add-on for $0.00 until the editor closes, so trying on and BUY work without a headset. You can also use **After Hours → Add-ons → Buy every add-on in the test store** and **Refund the test store's add-ons** (both in Play Mode).

**Ownership check.** Meta requires every store app, free ones included, to check within ten seconds of launch that the player has it. If the check says no, a release build closes; development builds and the editor only log it. If the Platform SDK can't start at all, the game keeps running without add-ons rather than closing.

**Adding a new outfit for sale.**
1. Build its look on the avatar in `WorldBuilder.Avatar` (or `Legwear` for legs, hands and feet) and add it to `Outfits.All` with `Sold(id, name, slot)`. Never rename an id once it has shipped.
2. Create the add-on in the dashboard with exactly its SKU (below).
3. Rebuild the scene (After Hours → Build or rebuild the game scene).

**Set up in the Meta developer dashboard** (developers.meta.com/horizon/manage, app After Hours, id 1393348977192544):
1. **Pricing**: set the app itself to free when you're ready to release it as free to play.
2. **Requirements → Data Use Checkup**: under the platform features, request **User ID** and **In-App Purchases**, choosing *Use Add-ons: Downloadable Content and In-App Purchases Commerce (IAP)* as the usage. Answer the data handling questions and submit. An organization admin has to submit it, and Meta reviews it; purchases don't work until it is approved.
3. **Monetization → Add-ons → Create Add-on**, once for each row of `Documentation/AddOns-catalog.tsv`: the SKU exactly as listed (case-sensitive), type **Durable**, the name, a short description, a price (USD) and an icon. The game shows the price from here; the checkout shows the name and icon from here.
4. Upload a build containing this code to a release channel (Alpha is fine), then buy an add-on on the headset with a tester account. Use a test user or a test payment method from the dashboard to avoid real charges.

The checks: **After Hours → Tests → Run outfit add-on checks** (see Validation). **After Hours → Capture room previews** saves seven mirror stills that between them show every add-on.

## The menu

Press the left controller's **menu button**, or with tracked hands look at your left palm and pinch, to open the menu. Press it again, or select **CLOSE**, to put it away. Desktop preview uses Tab. If a message or the hint under it is up, that first press only clears it (see below); press again for the menu. The menu opens at arm's length, a little below eye level, wherever you are facing, and it moves and turns with you. It closes on its own when you change rooms.

| Row | Buttons |
|---|---|
| Turning | **< TURN**, **TURN AROUND**, **TURN >**, **FACE FORWARD** |
| Seated play | **SEATED VIEW**, **SEATED HEIGHT**, **MOVEMENT** |
| Comfort | **COMFORT VIGNETTE**, **KITCHEN CROWD**, **MUSIC** |
| Display | **CLEAN MODE**, **OCEAN AT NIGHT** |
| The ambush | **GENTLE AMBUSH**, **DARK OPENING** |
| Room | **TRY AGAIN** (in each chapter), **CLOSE**, **LOBBY** (outside the lobby) |

### Clearing the screen, clean mode and night

Some messages and signs used to stay up until something else replaced them. Now **the menu button closes everything**: one press clears the message in the air, the hint under it and the menu, and never opens the menu on that same press. The first-visit card is the one exception while it waits for you to open the menu: there the press opens it, so the guide can go on.

**CLEAN MODE** goes further and turns everything off: every room title, caption, counter, instruction and the menu hint, and the first-visit card. Buttons keep their labels, the dark plates that some signs sit on go away with their text, the rooms look the same and the comfort menu stays reachable. It is saved, so it is still on at the next launch. Select it again to bring everything back.

**OCEAN AT NIGHT** changes only the ocean of shame: a dark, starlit sky with a moon, dim moonlight on the water and boat, a glowing lantern, a stronger lighthouse beam, wind over the water and a bell buoy. Every other room keeps its own light, and the sea goes back to night whenever you return to it. It is saved too.

### Music

Calm music plays quietly under every room's own sound: the after hours theme in the lobby and on the rooftop, and a slower, softer piece (*Quiet hours*) in the chapters. It carries on across room changes, swapping pieces while the screen is dark, and each piece picks up where it was left. **MUSIC** in the menu fades it out over a second and a half (and back in), and the choice is saved, so it stays off at the next launch. The rooms' ambience is not affected. The menu's bottom row was already as low as is comfortable, so the panel grew upward for the new button: the turning buttons and FACE FORWARD now sit just above eye level.

All the settings used to be signs in the lobby, and each chapter had LOBBY and TRY AGAIN signs. Those signs are gone. The lobby keeps its doors and CONTINUE, and every chapter keeps its own task buttons (ROW, BREW, ASK FOR HELP).

## Seated play and short sessions

Select **SEATED VIEW** in the menu to calibrate a seated view. **SEATED HEIGHT** cycles between 1.45, 1.65 and 1.85 metres (from standing, the first press switches seated view on). This raises the view and tracked hands together, and saves the chosen default for the next launch. Switch seated view off and on again to recalibrate after changing chairs or posture. Standing view restores the headset's tracked height.

Movement is set up so you never have to twist in the chair:

- **Snap turns.** The right stick turns 30 degrees. Pull it straight back to turn around. With walking off (**MOVEMENT: TELEPORT ONLY**), and in the boat, the left stick turns too. With tracked hands, use the turn buttons in the menu.
- **Teleports turn you.** In seated view, a floor teleport turns you to face the way you pointed, so you can turn by teleporting.
- **FACE FORWARD** turns the room so its main view is straight ahead again, wherever your chair points.
- **Comfort vignette.** The edges of your view soften while you walk with the stick, and clear when you stop. **COMFORT VIGNETTE** switches it off. The setting is saved.

**Every chapter can be finished from the seat you arrive in, within arm's reach.** Nothing has to be walked across a room:

- **Pick things up from where you sit.** Point at an object up to 10 m away and pinch (or grip). It glides to your hand. The pointer leans toward the nearest object within a few degrees, so a shaky hand ray still finds it.
- **Send things where they belong.** While holding something, point at its destination and let go. The destination lights up while you point at it (the truck's bay, the spout, each archive tray), and the object glides there in a short arc. A note sent to the wrong tray drifts back to the desk.
- **The boat rows with a button**: ROW sweeps both oars through a full stroke, the same as rowing by hand.
- **The kitchen crowd stays in front of you.** Watchers gather on both sides of the coffee machine, within 60 degrees of it, so a seated player sees them without turning round.
- **The shredder is close.** It stands on your side of the desk, a step or two to the right of the arrival point and turned to face it, clear of the line of sight to the pages and the four true lines. A page can still be sent to it from the seat, or thrown.
- **The ambush is arranged for a seat.** You are in a chair at the end of the table. The six feeling cards, the five replies, the paperwork and the balloons and noses are all pointed at from where you sit, and the clowns are on the far side of the table, so a page is sent to a clown by pointing and letting go, never by walking. STAND UP appears on the table close in front of you. Nothing in the room needs the player to move.
- **The mirror comes to you.** MIRROR on the desk teleports you to the glass, and every wardrobe button can be pointed at from there.
- **The rage room is sized for a seat.** The computer is on a desk just in front of you: every part is within 70 cm of the right hand of a seated player, well inside the bat's 85 cm, and the monitor and keyboard are within reach of either hand. The five desk things are within 80 cm of a seated hand too. Swing speed is measured at the bat's tip and smoothed over a few frames, so a quick flick of the forearm counts as a full hit while tracking jitter and slow movements never do. Strength only changes how loud the hit sounds. The bat stays in your hand when the pinch opens, and through a moment of lost tracking during a fast swing; PUT THE BAT BACK, or leaving the room, returns it to its rack.

The **airplane-seat test** (below) checks this: it completes every chapter from its arrival point without moving the player once. Comfort and readability still need checking on a physical headset.

For a short visit, row a few strokes, deliver one box, sort one note, or make one coffee step, then select LOBBY in the menu or close the app. Resume later without repeating those completed steps.

## Hands, controllers and Quest 2

**Tracked hands are the first and default way to play.** Nothing has to be set: the game starts in hands wording, and follows whatever the headset reports. Put the controllers down and use your hands, and every sign, hint and the first-visit guide talk about pinching and palm pinches. Pick the controllers up and, after about half a second, they talk about the trigger, the grip and the menu button instead. Signs with two wordings (the lobby board, the comfort note and the menu's tip) change in place; the rest say the right thing the next time they appear. There is no setting to find.

With controllers, the pointer works as before (aim and squeeze the trigger to choose), and **the trigger now also picks things up**, just as a pinch does, so one finger does everything; the grip still works too. Letting go of whichever you used drops, sends or throws.

**A thumb shortcut for the lobby.** With tracked hands there is a quick way back that needs no reaching: on a Quest that supports Meta's thumb microgestures (the Quest 3 and 3S), rest a hand in a relaxed curl and **swipe your thumb back along the side of your index finger**. The game asks "Back to the lobby? Tap your thumb on the side of your finger to go. Swipe forward to stay." Then **tap your thumb against the side of your finger** within four seconds and you are back in the lobby, with the room you left saved for CONTINUE. Any other thumb swipe, or four seconds with no tap, means stay. A tap on its own does nothing, and in the lobby, or while you are moving between rooms, the swipe does nothing either; it asks once more so that a stray swipe cannot send you anywhere. Either hand works. It does what the right controller's B button does, which hands had no way to do. With CLEAN MODE on the question is not written out, but the same soft sounds as the menu mark the question and the answer. `ThumbShortcuts` reads Meta's `OVRHand.GetMicrogestureType()` for both hands every frame; there is no setting to find.

**One APK for Quest 2, Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S.** The Android manifest lists all four, hand tracking is optional so a headset without it still starts, and the game needs no passthrough, scene or camera permissions. On a Quest 2 the game notices the headset and asks for a lighter picture: render scale 0.9, shadows to 20 m and a 72 Hz display (only if the headset offers it). Every headset also gets **foveated rendering**: the game asks the headset to shade the outer part of each eye's picture more coarsely, where the lens is soft anyway, and the time saved goes to a steadier frame rate. The level is a ceiling that the headset adapts to the load (dynamic foveation), so it uses none while the scene is cheap to draw and reaches for more only when it is running short of time. The ceiling is High on a Quest 2 and Medium on a Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S; a headset the game does not recognise, or a runtime without the feature, is left alone, and the game logs a "DeviceProfile" line saying what was asked and what the headset reported back. Nothing about how the game plays changes. See `Documentation/Compatibility.md` for what has and has not been measured; **the Quest 2 profile is unmeasured**, because it has only been exercised on a Quest 3 and in the editor, and **foveated rendering has not yet been judged by eye on a headset**: the editor cannot show it, and whether the edges look soft enough to notice is for the playtest to say.

## Quest build

There are two build commands in the **After Hours** menu:

- **Build shareable Quest APK (release)** (one APK for Quest 2, Quest Pro, Quest 3 and Quest 3S) writes `Builds/Quest/AfterHours-release.apk`. Give this one to playtesters. It leaves out Meta's XR Operator test layer and the Immersive Debugger, and it clears the DevAgent's network address and access token from the build.
- **Build Quest APK (development)** writes `Builds/Quest/AfterHours.apk`. It includes Meta's test tooling for Meta XR Simulator and agent-driven testing, so keep it on your own headset. In this build, clicking the left thumbstick toggles the Immersive Debugger. The left menu button stays reserved for the game's menu, and B/Y for returning to the lobby.

To play on your own headset, connect the Quest with a USB data cable, turn on Developer Mode, and accept **Allow USB debugging** in the headset. Then use **After Hours → Install release APK on connected Quest** (or the development equivalent). It installs the APK with Unity's bundled `adb`, starts the game, and reports each step in the Console. If the headset hasn't been authorized, more than one device is connected, or the APK doesn't exist yet, the Console says so.

## Release signing

The Meta Developer Dashboard only accepts APKs signed with your own key. **After Hours → Create release signing key** makes one, once, with the JDK that ships with Unity: `UserSettings/AfterHours-release.keystore` and a random password in `UserSettings/AfterHours-release-key.txt`. `UserSettings` is never committed. The release build signs with this key and puts the project back on the debug key afterwards, so no keystore path or password stays in the project settings. The development build stays debug-signed.

**Back up both files somewhere private.** An app on the Meta store can only ever be updated with the key it was first uploaded with.

Every build gets a new version code: the number of minutes since January 1, 2026, and always higher than the last build's. The version name is 0.8.3. The dashboard rejects an upload whose version code it has seen before.

**After Hours → Commit and push to GitHub…** commits everything git doesn't ignore and pushes the current branch to `origin`. The shareable release APK is committed too, through Git LFS, so playtesters can download `Builds/Quest/AfterHours-release.apk` from GitHub. Development APKs, `Library`, removed packages and the Immersive Debugger's per-computer settings (`Assets/Resources/DevAgentSettings.asset`, which holds this computer's network address and an access token) stay out of the repository. The push stops if GitHub has commits this computer doesn't, and the first push may open Git Credential Manager's GitHub sign-in window. Commits use the name and email under **Commit as**. Your GitHub account keeps its email private, so use your GitHub noreply address there; GitHub refuses pushes that show the private one.

Both build commands build from the Android platform settings. If a custom build profile such as Unity's **Meta Quest** profile is active, they switch back to the platform profile first, because a custom profile carries its own player, quality and XR settings.

Both commands apply the Quest settings first: OpenXR with Meta XR, controllers and hands, ARM64, IL2CPP, Vulkan, linear color, 4x MSAA, render scale 1.0, 28 m shadow distance, and Android API 32 minimum. Mixed-reality features stay off, so the APK requests no scene, anchor, passthrough or headset-camera permissions. The target API is Android 34, which the Meta Quest Store and its release channels expect, and dynamic resolution is on, as Meta's Project Setup Tool recommends. The release APK is signed with your release key (see above); the development APK with Unity's debug key. The project doesn't include the Meta Voice SDK, Meta XR Audio or Unity IAP packages. The game doesn't use them, and the Voice SDK's build step crashed the Android build. Only the current room is active. Static geometry is combined by material. Anything the player can pick up, or that moves or switches on and off, keeps its own mesh, and the scene builder logs an error if a movable object is ever merged.

## Art style

The game uses a refined low-poly look:

- Every box has a small bevel. The bevel stays the same size in metres however the box is scaled, so edges catch the light the same way on a keyboard key and on a wall.
- Cylinders, spheres and capsules are faceted, and every facet is flat shaded.
- Materials are matte flat colours in the midnight, teal, coral and brass palette. There are no photographic textures. The rooftop alone uses a brighter, more saturated toy palette (tomato, sunshine, sky blue, leaf green, lilac and pink on warm cream) with rounder, chunkier shapes.
- Floors are laid as bevelled tiles, walls as panels and ceilings as coffers.
- Gradient ambient light makes up-facing facets lighter and down-facing ones darker.
- Text is kept readable. Signs stand where nothing in the room can pass between them and a player (the lighthouse and its beam, the lobby counter, shelves and the truck included), anything that has to be read against a busy background sits on its own dark plate, and light lettering has a thin dark outline. The text visibility checks above verify this for every sign.

Tiles and panels don't cast their own shadows. One invisible, square-edged slab per surface casts the shadow instead, so no light leaks through the seams. Floors cast no shadows.

**The sea moves.** The ocean's water is still one low-poly mesh of flat facets (32 by 44 squares, two metres each) lit by the same matte water material by day and by night, but `OceanWaves` rebuilds its heights thirty times a second from four crossing swells: a main swell of 24 m length and an eight second beat, a longer one underneath, a cross swell and a little chop, a little under half a metre from trough to crest all together. Every facet is lit afresh as it tilts, so the light slides over the water. The player and the boat never move with it (heaving is a fast way to feel unwell in a headset), no wave climbs above the deck, and the water casts no shadows. The rebuild is about 1,500 height samples and 2,800 triangles, a third of a millisecond in the editor, and it runs only while the ocean room is on. The main swell's beat is the beat of the `Ambience - Waves` sound: `SeaSounds` keeps a clock that follows the sound's own position, and the water reads that clock, so a crest passes the boat as the sound crests.

**Loose paper.** Shredding a page is three layers of paper, none of them costly. The original falling strips drop from the slot into the bin. A flurry of loose bits (two particle systems, white and ivory, about 160 bits each a page) bursts up out of the head, tumbles on noise and spreads over the room, and comes to rest on a floor plane (no world collision, so no cost to find the floor). A moment later, once the flurry has come down, that page's set of scraps appears where the bits lay: about 240 thin flecks and strips of paper, thickest around the shredder and the desk, with more across the rest of the floor, on the desk either side of the page rack, on the shredder's top clear of the slot and on the window sill. Every set, and every heap in the bin, is merged into one mesh per paper colour, so each set is two draws and none has a collider, and a set is hidden until its page is shredded. The flurry's bits are thin boxes, so they have no shadows. If the app is closed midway, the scraps of every page already shredded are there when it reopens.

**Confetti.** The old résumé room has an easter egg. A page that hits the floor hard enough (at least 1.5 m/s, within 20 cm of the floor, on a flat knock; a page set on the desk or lowered gently does nothing) vanishes in a burst of confetti. Two one-shot particle systems are moved to where it landed and played: a burst of 220 flat bits that flies up and out from the page and slows in the air, and a shower of about 150 more that falls from two metres overhead. The bits are thin cuboids in seven colours (pink, amber, green, blue, violet, orange and yellow), each taking one at random from the system's start-colour gradient, so the whole system shares one flat unlit material and is one draw, with no shadows. They tumble, drift on a little noise, come to rest on a floor plane (no world collision), and shrink away at the end of their eight seconds or so. A pop with a flutter of paper and four bell notes plays where the page landed (`SFX - Confetti`, generated by `Tools/make_audio.py` like every other sound). The page comes back to its stand 1.6 seconds later, set down gently, and nothing is counted, so a chapter is never affected. Pages are thrown like the heavy room's boxes: a throw aimed within about 25 degrees of the shredder is pulled the rest of the way in (fading out by 55 degrees), and a page that reaches the slot's trigger is shredded and counted exactly as one sent there. A throw aimed anywhere else is left alone, so a page flicked at the floor still bursts, and one that lands somewhere else (on the desk, say) goes back to its stand after a moment. Letting go while pointing at the shredder with a still hand still sends the page in. Leaving the room mid-burst brings the page back at once, and TRY AGAIN in the room clears the confetti.

**Box blast.** The heavy room has a second easter egg. Hold a box in each hand and slam them together, and both burst. A held box is carried by the hand rather than moved by physics, so two held boxes never collide on their own; `BoxBlast` watches each carried box instead. After every box has followed its hand for the frame it works out how fast each is moving (smoothed over about 50 ms, so one jittery frame is not a slam) and bursts a pair when they are held in different hands, their colliders overlap, and they are closing on each other at 2.4 m/s or more. A box has to have been in the hand for at least 0.3 s, so one drawn in from across the room is never mistaken for a slam, and boxes held apart, pressed gently together, or carried side by side (however fast) do nothing. Four one-shot particle systems are moved to the midpoint and played: a flash (a warm cream ball, a fifth of a second, one pulse and never a strobe), 70 sparks (four fire colours, stretched along their flight), 9 puffs of dust-grey smoke and 110 chips of box that fly out, fall, bounce on a floor plane (no world collision) and lie there a moment before they shrink away. The chips come in the boxes' own colours with the tape and the plinth wood (coral, navy, teal, gold, metal, cream and wood), and all four systems share one flat unlit material, with no shadows and no more than 300 bits at once. `SFX - Blast` (a crack, a low boom, a rumble, a spray of sparks and a rattle of debris, 2.2 seconds, generated by `Tools/make_audio.py` at the end of the list so every earlier sound regenerates as it was) plays where they met. Both boxes are back on their stands, set down gently, two seconds later, and nothing is counted, so the chapter and its count are untouched. Leaving the room mid-blast brings the boxes back as soon as it reopens, and TRY AGAIN in the room brings them back at once and clears the smoke.

**Mugs, cupboards and the espresso machine.** The kitchen has five mugs, each a flat-bottomed faceted cup with a handle, a dark inside and a coffee disc that shows once something has been poured: your cup (white), a teal, a coral and a navy mug on the counter, and an orange mug on the shelf of the fifth cupboard from the left. Each is a `Grabbable` (carried and sent as before; the spout is one `SendTarget` that takes any mug by its `category`, and sending a mug there first sends whichever mug is already resting there back to where it started, so two never end up in each other) with a `Mug`. A mug is 'tossable' like the ambush paperwork, so a flick of the hand throws it, but pointing at the spout and letting go still sends it. A hard knock breaks it: `Mug` shatters when something hits it at 2.6 m/s or faster relative speed, which is a fall of about a third of a metre onto the counter or more, so setting a mug down never breaks it while a drop from carrying height to the floor (about 5 m/s) or a flick of the wrist always does. A hand sweeping through a mug, one mug gliding in beside another, and the first 0.4 s after a mug lands from a send are never knocks, and two mugs pressed into each other part gently (the limit is not saved with the scene, so `Mug` sets 1.5 m/s when it wakes). A breaking mug plays one of two shard systems (34 chips each, one that lands on a counter plane and one on a floor plane, tinted the mug's colour and white), a puff of 6 dust clouds and, if it held coffee, one of four coffee splashes that lie flat on whatever it hit, with `SFX - Shatter` where it broke. It is hidden and emptied, a gentle message says it is back on its shelf and that nothing is lost (at most once every ten seconds), and 2.5 s later it is set back whole (`Grabbable.BringBack`), so the pours, the mistakes and the chapter are untouched. Leaving the room mid-wait brings the mugs back as the room reopens, and TRY AGAIN brings them back at once, empties them, shuts the cupboards and clears the splashes. The six cupboards under the counter are hollow, and each door (`CabinetDoor`) hangs from a hinge on its outer edge and swings 100 degrees out into the room when it is pointed at and pressed, with `SFX - Cabinet` (a creak and a knock), and shuts when pressed again; the other five hold plates, jars, bowls, tins, a kettle and folded cloths, for looking at, and each says so. The orange mug waits near the front of its shelf, where a seated player can see it once the door is open. Nothing is counted for finding it. Filling it earns the Orange mug pin: pouring into it is an ordinary pour while the three are not yet done, and once all three are done and help has been asked for it can still be filled once, with 'ORANGE, AT LAST' on the machine and a message that someone kept it for you. The machine gained a lever that pulls down as it brews (60 degrees in 0.9 s, or a short 20 degree jerk when the pressure is faulty or no cup is in place), a pressure gauge, a portafilter handle, a steam valve and a steam wand with a nozzle. Pointing at the lever brews exactly as BREW does, and pointing at the wand or the valve blows a plume of steam (a particle system emitting about 26 puffs a second for a couple of seconds, with `SFX - Steam`) even while the pressure is faulty. Each new pointer is an invisible solid box on the machine, so the ray tests and the text visibility checks see them. The three new sounds are generated at the end of `Tools/make_audio.py`, so every earlier sound regenerates as it was (47 WAV files in all).

**The ambush.** The seventh chapter is one boardroom, 16 metres wide, 15 long and 10 high, with a night window at the far end, a table 9 metres long and your teal chair at the near end of it. `AmbushRoom` runs it. **The dark.** The room is switched on at once but the player's fade is held fully black (`AmbushRoom` sets it every frame, so the room is not just fading in), and only a boardroom tone (`Ambience - Boardroom`) is heard. After three seconds the lights come on with `SFX - Lights` and a flicker: the view is clear for 0.07 s, black for 0.14, clear for 0.06, black for 0.30, clear for 0.45 and black for 0.09, then stays clear, about four seconds after you arrived. Opening the menu, naming a card, replying, popping a balloon, honking a nose or standing up while it is dark ends the dark at once (and the action that did it is not counted), and **DARK OPENING** in the menu switches the dark off so the lights are already on when you arrive. The room is lit cool and harsh, with a grey-blue sky, a cold sun and the fog of the lobby, and everything shared by the whole scene (sky, ambient light and sun) is saved on arrival and put back as you leave, as it is when you stand up and the room turns warm: an amber sky, a lower sun, two table lamps and a dawn at the window. **The clowns.** Two giants, one coral and one teal, each a faceted body with a head, a red nose, brows, a collar, a bowtie, a propeller beanie, three balloons on strings and a chest with an invisible aim volume, stand across the table at 2.8 times life size (1.9 in a gentle ambush), 2.6 metres either side of the middle, leaning 11 degrees in over the table and turned toward you, so their heads are over five metres up. Their size, position, lean and a slow sway (which quiets with each feeling named) are set every frame from how many feelings are named: each one shrinks them at 0.9 of a size a second, straightens them and brings them in until, at six, they are 0.8 times a person, 1.3 metres either side, upright and still, and the screen between them says only “...”. The beanie appears from the second feeling and the bowtie from the fourth. A hit, a reply, a balloon or a honk makes the clown concerned flinch back for half a second. They are merged into one mesh per material when the scene is built, like the rest of the room, except everything under the room's moving parts (the clowns, the cards, the papers, the chair, the lamps and the way out), which keeps its own mesh and is named for its clown. **What you say.** The six cards and the five replies are `Interactable`s lying on the table or standing on the stairs, pointed at like every other button. A card names a feeling (once: pointing at one already named says so and counts nothing), turns pale with NAMED under its words and earns a kind line, and the room's count and the screen show it; the replies are answered by the left clown, the right clown or both with a line from a list of company phrases, and count for nothing. **The paperwork.** The six pages are `Grabbable`s that can be tossed (`tossable`) but are never helped toward a target. Pointing at a clown while holding one lights the clown's outline and, when you let go, sends the page there (a `SendTarget` in each clown's chest with the category `paper`, which the pointer can see through the table's own screen); a page thrown or dropped into a clown's chest hits it too, and `AmbushRoom` also watches every page for the chest's volume, so one carried through it counts. A hit or a tear says a line, flinches the clown and sends a puff of paper bits (46 white and ivory chips and flat bits) up from the spot, and the page is back on the table 1.8 seconds later, set down gently (`Grabbable.BringBack`). A page that is dropped off the table lies on the floor, out of a seated player's reach, and glides back after four seconds. To tear a page, shake it hard: `AmbushRoom` records where the held page has been over the last tenth of a second along the line between your shoulders, and it tears when that speed passes 1.6 metres a second in one direction and then the other four times in a row, each reversal within 0.7 seconds of the last. Shaking it up and down, or waving it slowly, never tears it; a torn page bursts into bits of paper with `SFX - Tear` and comes back. The balloons are `Interactable`s too (six of them on strings above the clowns, popped in a burst of 40 bits in the balloon's own colours with `SFX - Balloon`), the noses squeak (`SFX - Squeak`), and the clowns say a line the first time each is used. **Standing up.** When all six are named, STAND UP appears on the table close to the seat; pointing at it rolls your chair back, turns the light warm, lights the lamps, opens the way out and completes the chapter, with `SFX - Stand` and the Stood up pin. The room saves how many feelings are named (as a mask of the six cards, so each is restored exactly once) and your two choices with the rest of the checkpoint (`AfterHours.Progress.v1` gained bit 8, so seven chapters add up to 478), and a half-named room opens lit, the clowns the size you left them. TRY AGAIN, or leaving and returning, resets the feelings, the replies, the pages and the balloons; a finished chapter stays finished, and opens on the warm ending. The menu gained a row for the two ambush choices, so `ComfortMenu` lowers its panel a little (the drop went from 0.20 to 0.16 metres) to keep its bottom row within 42 degrees of straight ahead.

**The sunrise rooftop.** Room for tomorrow (room 5) was restyled in version 0.8.1 for a bright, saturated, chunky, toy-like look: a rounded, cheerful, high-colour kind of world, not copied from any game. Its layout and the three lines on its board are unchanged. **The sky.** `After Hours/Sunrise sky` (`Assets/AfterHours/Shaders/SunriseSky.shader`) is a skybox shader written for the project. It has no texture, so there is nothing to band, stream or seam, and the whole sky is one cheap pass worked out per pixel from the direction you are looking: gold at the horizon, then peach, pink, soft blue and morning blue straight up, a warm glow along the horizon that is strongest toward the sun, and below the horizon a pale peach. The sun is a flat disc 4.4 degrees in radius (pale in the middle, with a golden rim) in a tight halo and a wide soft one, 24 degrees to the right of the board and 13 degrees above the horizon (`RooftopRoom.SunAzimuth`, `SunElevation` and `SunRadius`). A whisper of noise (within half an 8-bit step) breaks up the colour steps. The shader sets up its stereo eye index the way the comfort vignette's does. **The light.** `RooftopRoom` sits on the room's root, so Unity switches it on when the player arrives and off when they leave (`OnEnable` and `OnDisable`). On arrival it records the scene's sky, ambient light colours, haze and directional light (the same things the ocean's night and the ambush change) and sets the sunrise: the sun a warm #FFD8A0 at intensity 1.25 and low in front of the player, ambient colours of pale lilac from above (#C4C6F6), peach at the horizon (#F7CBBC) and terracotta from below (#DB9E82), and a peach haze (#FFD2AA) that starts 34 metres away and is solid at 135, so the skyline stands out of the dawn instead of fading into the day's grey-green. On leaving it puts back exactly what it recorded, before the next room's own arrival code runs, so no other room is changed; the gameplay journey checks both ways. **The deck.** The floor is a 17 by 19 metre sunrise-coloured tile deck inside cream parapets with sky-blue caps (a new back wall behind the arrival point included), with a striped rug under the arrival point (its three layers all lower than five centimetres, so nothing stops a foot or the pointer), the board with its navy face and a golden trim, wooden legs and a row of little bulbs, bunting (eight poles, three strings across the deck and one down each side at 5.3 metres, a pennant about every 0.6 metres in tomato, sunshine, sky blue, leaf green, lilac and pink), two benches with backs at the sides, six potted plants, two deck chairs turned toward the sun and a side table with a glass of water, flower boxes on the parapets, a stairwell hut with a blue door and a porthole behind and to the left of the arrival point, a wooden water tower on stilts behind and to the right, and four beanbags near the rug that have no collider, so they can be walked through. Everything that is near the player and in the way of the board's lettering is low (under about 1.2 metres) or at the sides, the bunting strings are kept above the lines of sight, and the text visibility suite checks it all. **The city.** Four rings of buildings, at 30, 44, 60 and 76 metres around the deck, in six chunky pastel colours with roof caps, stairwells, lit and glass windows (on the two nearer rings), wooden water towers with cone roofs and, away from the sun only, aerials. Towards the sun the buildings are capped lower so the sun stays in view. The far buildings use their own materials and cast no shadows, so they are cheap to draw and cheap for the checks to ray-cast. **What moves.** Ten soft pink and white clouds drift across the sky at their own speeds and wrap around at 120 metres, and a striped hot-air balloon bobs up and down by up to 0.9 metres on an 18 second cycle while it turns slowly. They are kept under the room's `moving` group, which the scene builder's merging leaves alone (`Dynamic()`), so they keep their own meshes; everything else in the room is merged into one mesh per material as before. A rooftop preview needs no Play Mode: `RooftopRoom.Preview()` puts the sunrise in and `EndPreview()` puts everything back.

**After Hours → Capture room previews** renders a still from each room's arrival point into `Documentation/Previews/`, plus the menu in the lobby and the kitchen, the walking vignette, the first-visit card, each chapter's destinations lit up as a seated player sees them, the rage room's computer from the seat, and the mirror with four different looks. The ocean is captured by day and by night, with the oars and the lighthouse light, and the menu with its CLEAN MODE and OCEAN AT NIGHT buttons. The mirror and wardrobe suite saves two stills of the X-ray (the renderer only catches up with the switch while the game is running, so these are taken in Play Mode). The kitchen is captured with the whole machine (lever, gauge, steam valve and wand, mugs) and with the orange cupboard open, and the mirror with the Orange mug pin. The ambush is captured nine ways: the clowns looming, the table, the left clown, the paperwork, three feelings named, all six named, standing up, the way out, and a gentle ambush. The rooftop is captured twelve ways, from the arrival point and then in its sunrise look: the sunrise itself, the skyline on the left and on the right, the deck, the board, the deck chairs, the bunting, the sky, the stairwell hut, the water tower and the balloon. The gameplay journey also saves stills of the rage room with the bat in hand, broken, and quiet, and of the kitchen with the cupboard open. Art and comfort changes can be checked without a headset.

## More rooms to add

1. **The elevator of comparison**: every floor appears to be someone else's promotion. Choose a destination using your own values instead of chasing the highest number.
2. **The meeting that never ends**: chairs repeat imagined criticisms. Move one chair into daylight and replace guesses with things you actually know.
3. **The server room of rumination**: replaying conversations power an increasingly noisy machine. Unplug one loop at a time and route attention to an ordinary present-tense task.
4. **The lost-and-found of identity**: find parts of yourself that never appeared on a job description—friend, maker, parent, neighbor, learner—and arrange them on an empty name badge.
5. **The greenhouse of small beginnings**: plant modest intentions. Growth depends on returning gently, not speed, streaks, or productivity.
6. **The interview mirror**: practice telling your story while the reflection changes from an idealized employee into an ordinary, complete person.
