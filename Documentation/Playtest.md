# Headset playtest

Two passes. The first is the competition's own check: hands only, from an airplane seat. The second covers controllers, standing play and saving. If you have a Quest 2, run both passes on it as well; see the end of this page. Every chapter can be left with LOBBY in the menu, and the rooftop is open before any puzzle is finished.

Before starting, uninstall any older copy, then install the release APK (**After Hours → Install release APK on connected Quest**). To see the first-visit card again on a headset that has already played, clear the app's data in the headset's settings, or uninstall and reinstall.

## Pass 1: hands only, airplane seat

Sit in an upright chair with no table in front of you. Keep your elbows near your sides: imagine a seat back in front of you and neighbours on both sides, so nothing further than about 60 cm (2 ft) from your chest. Put the controllers out of reach before launching.

1. **First visit.** The card in the lobby should be readable without leaning. Read the content note, then pinch CONTINUE and GOT IT. Look at your left palm and pinch: the menu opens and the card moves on by itself. Close the menu and pinch START.
2. **Menu.** Open it again with a left palm pinch. Check that every button is readable and inside your view without turning your head. Select SEATED VIEW and try SEATED HEIGHT. Use TURN AROUND and FACE FORWARD. Close it.
3. **Ocean.** Pinch ROW until you reach the shore (fourteen strokes): both oars should sweep through the water by themselves. Then pinch an oar by its handle inside the boat, push it forward and draw it back once. The blade should swing the opposite way outside the boat, and the oar should stay in its lock. Watch the lighthouse light turn clockwise and listen for the sea and the gulls. The water should roll in long, slow swells that travel away from you toward the island, with a smaller cross swell and a little chop over them, and each time a big swell passes under the boat (about every eight seconds) you should hear a wave lift, crest with a hiss and slide away, in time with what you see. The boat and you stay still. As you row, the surf on the island's beach should get louder and clearer. Then open the menu, select LOBBY and go back into the ocean: after a finished crossing the boat should be out on open water again, the lighthouse far ahead, the counter reading 14 and both oars at rest. (If you leave halfway, it should carry on from where you were.)
4. **Heavy things.** For each box: point at it and pinch. It should glide to your hand. Point at the truck, check that the bay outline lights up, and open your fingers. The box should glide into the bay and count once. Check how forgiving the aim feels at the far side of the truck. Then pick up another box and throw it: open your fingers while your hand moves toward the truck. It should fly in an arc, land in the bay and slide into its place. Throw one the wrong way: it should fly where you threw it, then return to its stand with a gentle message. Check the throw feels like yours, and note whether the pull toward the truck feels too strong or too weak. For a small surprise, hold a box in each hand and slam them together: both should burst in a flash with sparks, smoke and chips in the boxes' colours and a boom, and be back on their stands about two seconds later. Say whether a real slam reliably does it and a gentle tap or carrying two boxes side by side does not, whether the burst looks good in the lenses, whether the smoke puffs and the flash, so close to your face, are comfortable, whether the boom is too loud or too quiet, and whether the frame rate holds.
5. **A small spill.** Pinch BREW twice. The watchers should appear at both sides of the machine, where you can see them without turning round, and the murmur should rise. Pinch ASK FOR HELP: the crowd leaves and the room goes quiet. Pick up the cup, point under the spout (the gold disc lights up), let go, and check it lands upright. Pinch BREW three times, using the lever on the side of the machine for one of them: it should swing down and come back up. Point at the steam wand, or the valve beside it, for a hiss and a plume of steam. Pick up another mug and send it under the spout the same way: the first mug should go back where it was. Then open the cupboards under the counter by pointing at each door and pinching: one of them (the fifth from the left) hides an orange mug. Take it, point under the spout, let go and pinch BREW: the Orange mug pin should be announced and be waiting at the mirror. Now the breakables: fling a mug at the floor, or lift one to chest height and let go. It should shatter with a crash, shards and a puff of dust (and a splash of coffee if it was full) and be back on its shelf about two seconds later, with nothing counted. Setting a mug down gently on the counter must never break it. Say whether the shatter is satisfying rather than startling, whether the mugs and the cupboard doors are easy to point at from your seat, whether throwing a mug feels like yours, how the steam and shards look in the lenses and whether the frame rate holds.
6. **The infinite archive.** Send one note to the wrong tray: it should drift back to the desk. Then file all six.
7. **The old résumé.** Send each page into the shredder: point at a page, pinch, point at the shredder (its slot lights up) and open your fingers. Check the page sinks into the slot, a flurry of paper bits bursts up out of the head and drifts down across the room, the paper in the bin grows, and a line answers each one. Look around once the paper has landed: scraps should lie on the floor all around the room, on the desk, on the top of the shredder and on the window sill, and each page should add more, so by the fifth the room is thick with paper. Say if it is too much or too little, if the thin bits flicker or shimmer in the lenses, or if you can see where a set of scraps pops in. For a small surprise, take a page and throw it onto the floor instead of at the shredder (let go while your hand is moving, with nothing pointed at): it should burst into confetti in many colours with a pop, and the page should be back on its stand about a second and a half later. Say how the confetti looks in the lenses (does the thin paper flicker, is the burst big enough to enjoy, is the shower too sparse or too heavy), whether the pop is too loud or too quiet, and whether a real throw reliably counts. A page set down gently should do nothing. When the blank page appears, pinch one of the four true lines.
8. **The mirror.** Open the menu, select LOBBY, and pinch MIRROR on the desk. Check you land facing the glass and your reflection follows your head and both hands, mirrored. Try every row of the wardrobe from where you sit, and put on the pieces you have earned. The line you chose should be above the glass.
9. **The rage room.** Point at the bat beside the desk and pinch: it should come to your hand and stay there when you open your fingers. Keeping your elbow near your side, flick your forearm at the monitor: a short, quick flick should land a full hit, with a sound, a jolt and lots of pieces thrown away from you. A slow push through it should do nothing. Break the monitor, keyboard, tower and mouse, and the mug, phone, stapler, pen cup and name plate on the desk (those are extras and do not count). Swing at pieces on the desk or floor: they should pop up and skid the way the bat goes, never back at you. Check every part can be reached with the bat without leaning, that the screen's messages change as the monitor cracks, and that the room goes quiet with the calm panel once everything is broken. Pinch WHEEL IN A NEW ONE, then PUT THE BAT BACK.
10. **The greenhouse.** Pinch WATER first: it should only say to plant a seed. Then send each seed packet to the planting bed: point at a packet on the bench, pinch, point at the bed (its outline lights up) and open your fingers. Each should go into a clay pot with a soft pat, and its intention should appear on a stake at the front of the pot. Pinch WATER, or point at the watering can and pinch: a shower should fall over the bed and every planted seed should come up as a sprout, and once all five are up the chapter completes. Open the menu, go to the LOBBY and come back: every plant should grow leaves, and the room should say you came back. Do it once more for flowers. Say whether the packets, the bed, WATER and the can are easy to point at from your seat, whether you can read the stakes from the bench, how the shower and the growing plants look in the lenses, and whether the rain on the glass is too loud or too quiet.
11. **Rooftop.** Pinch the rooftop door and stay for 20 seconds: an evening beanie should be announced.

Pass 1 passes if you finished everything without standing, leaning out of the imagined seat, reaching beyond the 60 cm, or picking up a controller. The bat may reach further than your hand; your arm should not. Note anything that needed a second try.

## Pass 2: controllers, standing, saving

1. Pick up the controllers and confirm the input changes without restarting. Open the menu with the left menu button. Try the right stick: snap turns, and pull back to turn around. With MOVEMENT set to TELEPORT ONLY, the left stick turns too. Teleport to one side and check you land facing where you pointed.
2. Walk with the left stick and check the comfort vignette softens the edges gently and clears when you stop. Try COMFORT VIGNETTE off.
3. Stand up, switch SEATED VIEW off, and check the tracked height returns.
4. In the warehouse, grab a box up close with the trigger and carry it into the bay by hand instead of sending it. Then throw boxes: let go of the trigger or grip while your hand moves toward the truck, from standing and from sitting, and check the controller buzz and the flight look right.
5. In the rage room, take the bat with the grip button and swing it with full, standing swings. Check hits still count once each, the controller buzzes on each hit (stronger for harder hits), and the bat stays in your hand through fast swings. Break everything and watch the frame rate: about 210 loose pieces are in play at once, so note the lowest figure.
6. Stop midway through each chapter, close the app, reopen it and select CONTINUE. Oar strokes, delivered boxes, sorted notes, shredded pages, broken computer parts, planted seeds and how far they have grown, help and coffee pours should all be kept. Loose objects may return to their starting places. Completed chapters stay marked, the first-visit card should not return, and the mirror should show what you were wearing.

7. **Messages, clean mode and night.** In any room with a message up, press the left menu button once: the message and the hint under it should go and the menu should stay shut. Press again: the menu opens. Select CLEAN MODE: every title, counter, caption and hint in the lobby and in each chapter should disappear while the buttons keep their labels. Close and reopen the app: it should still be clean. Turn it off and check everything returns. Then select OCEAN AT NIGHT, go to the ocean of shame, and check the sky, the moon, the dark water, the stronger beam and the bell buoy. Check the lobby and the other chapters are not dark.
8. **Rowing with controllers.** Grip an oar handle and push forward, then draw back, several times. Check the blade follows the right way, the stroke counts once per push-and-pull, the boat's progress feels earned and not too fast, and that letting go eases the oar back. Check that the ROW button can still be pointed at with an oar next to it.
9. **The mirror's secret.** Use MIRROR on the desk, then tap the glass three times (trigger or pinch, each tap within a couple of seconds of the last). Your reflection should become a white glowing skeleton on a dark backdrop that follows your head and hands. Three more taps, or leaving the lobby, bring the normal reflection back. Check the glow reads well against the dark and the skeleton keeps up with you.

10. **Hands to controllers and back.** Start with your hands only, then pick up the controllers: within about a second the lobby board, the comfort note and the first message you see should talk about the trigger and the menu button, not pinching. Put them down again: the wording should return to pinching. Note any flicker, and anything that still said the wrong thing.
11. **Quest 2.** On a Quest 2, repeat Pass 1 (hands) and Pass 2 (controllers). Check it installs and starts, the lobby and each chapter hold a steady frame rate (the profile asks for 72 Hz at render scale 0.9), nothing is too blurry to read at that scale, hand tracking works as it does on the Quest 3, and throwing and the oars feel the same. The Quest 2 profile is a guess that has not been measured; if it is too slow or too soft, say which room and what you saw.

12. **Reading everything.** In each room, from your seat, read every sign without leaning or moving: the heading, the instructions and the counters. Every sign sits on its plate with an even margin; look for words that touch an edge or float in a lot of empty plate. In the ocean, read the rowing note on your left, the title on your right and the distance counter while you row toward the lighthouse, and check the lighthouse or its beam never covers any of them. In the lobby, read the door signs, the tagline over the far wall and the rooftop sign at the far end. In the heavy room, read the heading on the back wall over the truck and the truck's sign. Note any sign that is hidden, too small, or too faint against what is behind it, with the room and where you were looking.

## What to record

- Quest model, seated or standing, hands or controllers.
- Frame rate: turn on the headset's performance overlay (Meta Quest Developer Hub, or OVR Metrics Tool) and note the lowest figure in each room. The target is a steady 72 fps or better.
- Any text you couldn't read, anything you couldn't reach, any discomfort, and any sound that was too loud, too quiet or missing (the waves, the surf, the gulls and the bell buoy in particular: the waves are the loudest part of the sea by day, so say if they are too loud or too quiet, and whether the water and the wave sound feel in step).
- How long the whole journey took. The competition judges play for up to ten minutes, so note how long each chapter takes on its own too.
