### Description (500 words suggested)

#### Inspiration

[Your own one or two sentences: why you made a game about losing a job. If it happened to you, say so here. This is the line nothing else in the entry can replace.]

Most games about hard feelings ask you to watch them. After Hours asks you to pick them up. Losing a job comes with shame, anger, worry and a lot of paperwork, and each of those can be a physical thing you hold, throw, shred or smash. Nothing is timed, nothing can be failed, and every chapter is built to be finished in one sitting.

#### What it is

From a quiet lobby you choose any of seven chapters, in any order:

- **Ocean of shame**: row fourteen strokes toward a lighthouse, with oars that turn in real oarlocks.
- **Heavy things**: anger, fear, relief, grief and uncertainty are boxes. Throw them on the truck. Every feeling is accepted.
- **A small spill**: the coffee machine leaks and a crowd gathers with each mistake, until you ask for help.
- **The infinite archive**: sort your thoughts into FACT, FEAR and STILL TRUE on a reading floor in endless stacks.
- **The old résumé**: shred the pages, then keep one line that is still true about you.
- **The rage room**: take a bat to the old work computer, which breaks into 152 loose pieces.
- **The ambush**: the lights stutter on and two clowns tower over a boardroom table, saying "This isn't personal." Name six feelings and they shrink until they are smaller than you. Then stand up.

A sunrise rooftop waits for rest whenever you want it, and a mirror in the lobby shows a wardrobe of fifteen pieces earned by playing. A board counts the days you come back and awards badges. One day off never ends a run, and nothing is ever taken away. Small secrets reward curiosity: slam two boxes together, or throw a mug.

#### How we built it

After Hours is built in Unity 6 with OpenXR and the Meta XR SDK v207. It was designed for one test: can every chapter be finished with hands, from a seat, without a controller? Pointing and pinching picks up anything within 10 m. Pointing at a destination and opening your fingers sends the object there, and a short flick of the forearm counts as a full bat swing.

All geometry, wording, music and sound were made for this project. The world is generated by a deterministic scene builder, and all 53 sounds are synthesised by our own script, with no samples. Development used AI coding agents and Meta's XR tools, and a desktop test suite of 1,245 automated checks plays every chapter from the seat and tests every sign for readability. Foveated rendering and a lighter profile for Quest 2 help hold the frame rate. [Add measured frame rates once you have them.]

### Hand interactions and the future of VR (optional)

Tracked hands are the default and only requirement. Controllers are noticed automatically if picked up, and the wording on every sign follows. A pinch along the hand ray selects and picks up, with the pointer leaning toward the nearest object so a shaky ray still lands. Opening the fingers sends, drops or throws according to where you point and how your hand is moving. A left palm pinch opens the menu. The rest is physical: push and draw an oar, flick a bat, slam two boxes together, shake a page hard until it tears.

## Risks

- **Repeat use is still the hardest thing to show.** The judges look for a "habit-forming purpose". The streak, badges and reward pins now give a reason to come back, but a judge sees one day of them, so the badge board has to appear in the video and the text. Present it as coming back when you need to, where a missed day costs nothing, and don't claim that returning improves anyone's wellbeing.
- **Don't claim platform features it doesn't use.** The game doesn't use gaze or passthrough, and the game's scripts don't use the Interaction SDK, even though the package is installed. Hands come through `OVRHand` and the game's own pointer. Fourteen days is too short to add these safely: a solid hands-only build will score better than a late feature.
- **Don't present it as therapy.** The game already says it is no substitute for professional support, and the text above makes no health claims. Keep it that way.
- **Quest 2 is listed in the manifest.** A judge could run it there. If it can't hold its frame rate after tuning, consider removing it from the supported list before the freeze.
